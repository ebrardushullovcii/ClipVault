#how-it-works

The buffer, the hotkey and the two audio tracks.

ClipVault

ClipVault runs in the tray and keeps a rolling buffer of your screen and audio. When you press your hotkey, the last 2 minutes are saved as an MP4 in your clips folder, and Windows shows a “Clip Saved” notification.

Saving a clip

  1. Play. The buffer keeps the last 2 minutes. You can change the length in Settings.
  2. Press your HOTKEY. It works while a fullscreen game has focus.
  3. Find it in the library. The clip is at the top, tagged with the game.

Game audio and your microphone are recorded on separate tracks, so you can turn your mic off or down later without touching the game sound.

#library

Where saved clips land.

ClipVault

New clips show up at the top of the library as soon as they're saved. Supported games are detected and tagged, and the file name includes the date, time and game.

In the library

  • Hover a card to play the clip in the grid.
  • Search, and sort by date, size, name or favorite.
  • Filter by game, tag or favorites. Grid or list view.
  • Select several clips to tag, favorite, export or delete them together.

#editor

Trim it, fix the audio, export it at a size you can post.

ClipVault

Open a clip to trim it, switch your mic track off, and export it at the size you need.

Editing and export

Trim
Drag the handles. Playback stays inside the trim.
Audio
Turn the game or mic track off, or change its volume.
Size
Up to 10 MB for Discord, 50 MB, 100 MB, or the original file.
Codec
H.264 plays everywhere. AV1 gives better quality per MB on NVIDIA RTX 40-series and newer.
After export
Drag the file out of the export window, copy its path or open its folder.
Also
Tags, favorites, frame stepping, and trimming the original file in place.

#capture

What it records, how it encodes, where the files go.

ClipVault

By default ClipVault records your monitor with DXGI. It doesn't hook into games or inject anything, which keeps it anti-cheat friendly.

Recording

Record
Your monitor by default. Game-only and monitor-plus-games modes are experimental. You can also pick an app window.
Encoder
NVENC on NVIDIA GPUs, x264 on the CPU otherwise. Auto picks for you.
Video
H.264 or AV1, 30 to 144 fps, five quality presets.
Files
Plain MP4s in a folder you choose. No account, nothing uploaded.

#faq

Before you install.

PinnedCommon questions

Is it free?

Yes. It's free and open source under GPL-2.0-or-later, and the code is on GitHub.

What do I need?

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. An NVIDIA GPU is used for NVENC encoding when you have one; otherwise it encodes with x264 on the CPU.

Which key saves a clip?

Any key you choose during setup or in Settings. It's F9 by default, changeable in Settings. It works while a fullscreen game has focus.

How long are clips?

As long as the buffer, 2 minutes by default. You can make the buffer longer or shorter, and trim any clip afterwards.

Will it cause anti-cheat problems?

It doesn't hook into games or inject anything. By default it records your monitor, the same way screen recording does.

Does it upload my clips?

No. There's no account, and clips are MP4 files in a folder you choose.

Does it start with Windows?

If you want it to. It runs from the tray, and the first run walks you through the clips folder, capture, audio and hotkey.

What's it built on?

libobs, the recording engine from OBS Studio, with an Electron and React app on top.
